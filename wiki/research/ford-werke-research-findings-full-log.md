# Ford-Werke Research Findings — Cumulative Log (Sections 1–13, Appendices A–I, Independent Assessment)

## Context
Full extraction and structured research log from *Research Findings About Ford-Werke Under the Nazi Regime* (Ford Motor Company, 2001) plus the accompanying Independent Assessment by Lawrence Dowler, Ph.D. (November 2001). Document processed page-by-page via targeted pdf_browse. All claims tagged Verified (secondary of primary). Primary archival references preserved. ## Payload

### Research Log Tables (cumulative)

#### Sections 1–8 (prior extraction summary)
- Establishment of Ford-Werke (1925 Berlin, 1931 Cologne); board composition including Charles E. Sorensen (1930–1941).
- Nazi economic controls and mobilization; military production (trucks, engines, components).
- Forced/foreign labor: Russian/Eastern workers from April 1942; Italian POWs from November 1943; foreign share rising to ~37% by August 1944. Appendix D monthly series.
- U.S.–Cologne communications constrained after 1941; limited technical exchanges.
- Occupied-Europe coordination (Paris, etc.).
- Trading with the Enemy Act probes and Edsel–Dollfus correspondence.

#### Section 9 — U.S. Investigations and Communications
- Trading with the Enemy Act inquiries; limited documentary evidence of ongoing control.
- Edsel Ford correspondence with Maurice Dollfus (Ford of France) regarding wartime conditions.

#### Section 10 — 1945 Capture, Military Government, Recovery
- Capture of Cologne plant; Erhard Vitger as U.S. Military Government custodian.
- Production recovery 1945–1949; denazification of personnel; Schneider Report referenced.
- Board reconstituted December 1947 (Henry Ford II, Ernest Breech, Russell Roberge).

#### Section 11 — War Damage Timeline and Claims
- Raid chronology and physical damage.
- Form 846 claim: total $12,461,427; Ford ownership share (56.575%) = $7,050,052 claimed (Appendix E).

#### Section 12 — Financial Continuity 1933–1953
- Balance sheets (Appendix F): assets RM 60.5M (1939) → RM 68.9M (1945) → DM 38.3M post-conversion → DM 102.6M (1953).
- Results of operations (Appendix G): sales peak wartime ~RM 161M (1943); net losses 1944–1945; postwar recovery and dividend resumption 1950.

#### Section 13 — Disposition of Research Findings
- ~98,000 pages entered into searchable database.
- Collection (privacy-restricted items excepted) donated to Henry Ford Museum & Greenfield Village / Benson Ford Research Center for public access.
- Bibliography in Appendix I.

#### Appendices A–I
- A: U.S. investment rankings in Germany (1943); Ford-Werke 16th ($8.55M, 1.90%).
- B: Board of Directors 1925–1953 (Sorensen 1930–1941; Albert chairman 1937–1947; 1942 board authority abolished).
- C: Allied war production (Ford U.S.: 8,700 B-24s, 282,400 jeeps, etc.).
- D: Monthly workforce series January 1941–December 1944 (foreign % 13% → 37%).
- E: Detailed war damage claims breakdown.
- F: Comparative balance sheets.
- G: Detailed results of operations 1933–1953.
- H: Repository prefix glossary (FMC, FW, NARA, PRO, HFM, BA-L, HStAD, ELDE, IWM, etc.).
- I: Bibliography (Wilkins & Hill, Nevins & Hill, Sorensen *My Forty Years with Ford*, Reich, Billstein et al., Rosellen, Overy, Herbert).

#### Independent Assessment (Lawrence Dowler, Ph.D., November 2001)
- Three-year external review of the 3½-year Archives Research Project (triggered by February 1998 BBC program and 4 March 1998 class-action suit).
- Five key findings: uncompromising multi-repository search; separation of legal and fact-finding tracks; sophisticated item-level database; Research Findings report preserves contradictory evidence; collection committed to independent repository before contents known.
- Process: Elizabeth Adkins (project manager); Dearborn, Washington (Winthrop/History Associates), and German (Lower Saxony Institute) teams; Cologne plant survey recovered >25,000 pages; PwC financial analysis (separate report).
- System of access: database, ~600-page chronology (1907–1967), eight white papers, Research Findings report.
- Gaps noted: limited individual forced-worker records; Cologne Labor Office files destroyed; conflicting end-of-war workforce counts.
- Conclusion: sets precedent for corporate archival openness.

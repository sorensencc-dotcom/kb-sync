### Findings Summary
# GAP-08 Findings: CIC - Ford Executive Dynamics & Politics (Klann Magneto Experiment Timeline)

## Research Focus
Reconstruct the exact chronological timeline of Klann's April 1, 1913 flywheel magneto experiment; verify plant superintendent Peter E. Martin's role/initiation.

## Summary of Public Sources (Finding Aids / Secondary + Cited Primary)

### Core Claim Confirmation (PARTIAL)
Multiple secondary and popular histories, plus Henry Ford's own published account, date the first experimental moving assembly line at Ford to the flywheel magneto department on or about **April 1, 1913**.

- **Henry Ford, *My Life and Work* (1922, with Samuel Crowther)**:  
  “Along about April 1, 1913, we first tried the experiment of an assembly line. We tried it on assembling the fly-wheel magneto.”  
  (Public domain text widely available via archive.org and other repositories. This is the most frequently cited primary published source for the date.)

- **Wikipedia “Assembly line”** (citing multiple secondary works and The Henry Ford archives):  
  Attributes the introduction of the assembly line idea to William “Pa” Klann after observing the Chicago meatpacking “disassembly line.” Klann reported the idea to Peter E. Martin (then rising to head of production), who was initially doubtful but encouraged the experiment. The magneto line is described as the first moving assembly line implemented.

- **Hemmings Motor News / other automotive histories**:  
  P.E. Martin served as plant superintendent. By 1913 he was officially superintendent of production with Charles E. Sorensen as assistant. Magneto assembly experiments occurred under this structure. Full chassis line followed later in 1913 (commonly cited as October 7, 1913 for the first continuous chassis line).

- **Oral history finding aid**:  
  “The Reminiscences of Mr. W. C. Klann” (Owen W. Bombard interviews, Accession 65, The Henry Ford, interview conducted September 1955). Description notes Klann describing the origin, development, and utilization of moving assembly lines for magnetos, motors, and final car assembly, and references to Peter E. Martin. Full transcript not freely readable online in this pass; held at The Henry Ford archives.

### Timeline Reconstruction (Inferred from Consistent Secondary Citations)
- Pre-1913: Stationary assembly; experiments with work flow and machine placement (Flanders et al.).
- ~1912–early 1913: Klann (and/or others) observe Chicago packing-house methods.
- **April 1, 1913 (approx.)**: First moving line trial on flywheel magneto assembly at Highland Park. Reported reduction in labor time for magneto assembly (from ~20 minutes to ~5 minutes in Ford’s account and derivatives).
- Mid-to-late 1913: Extension to other sub-assemblies (motor, transmission).
- October 7, 1913 (commonly cited): First continuous moving chassis assembly line.
- December 1913 onward: Further lengthening and refinement; by early 1914 production rates rise dramatically.

### Peter E. Martin Role
- Plant superintendent / production superintendent by 1913.
- Klann reported the packing-house idea to Martin; Martin encouraged the trial despite initial skepticism (Wikipedia synthesis of archive sources).
- Martin and Sorensen formed a long-running production partnership; Martin’s authority covered the Highland Park operations where the experiments occurred.
- Primary corporate records (Ford Motor Company archives, The Henry Ford) would be required to confirm exact internal memos, work orders, or payroll changes tied to the April 1 experiment. No such primary box contents were accessible or read in this remote pass.

### Open Questions / Residual Gaps
- Exact hour-by-hour or day-of-week corroboration beyond Ford’s “along about April 1.”
- Contemporary internal documents (time-study sheets, departmental reports) vs. later recollections.
- Possible contradictions between Klann’s 1955 oral history, Sorensen’s 1956 memoir *My Forty Years with Ford*, and Ford’s 1922 account regarding credit and sequence.
- Court dockets or accession filings: none identified in public finding aids that directly litigate or accession the 1913 magneto experiment itself.

## Verdict
**PARTIAL**. Date and magneto focus are consistently attested in Ford’s published memoir and secondary literature grounded in The Henry Ford oral histories and corporate records. Exact chronological micro-timeline and primary internal corroboration of Martin’s “initiation” role remain unconfirmed without direct reading of Accession 65 (Klann) or equivalent production records. No invention of box contents.

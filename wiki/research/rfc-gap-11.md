## Michigan Supreme Court Records – Dodge v. Ford Motor Co.

**Citation (primary published):** 204 Mich. 459; 170 N.W. 668 (1919).  
**Decision date:** February 7, 1919 (rehearing denied May 1, 1919).  
**Submitted:** April 9, 1918.  
**Appeal from:** Wayne Circuit Court (chancery).  
**Parties:** John F. Dodge and Horace E. Dodge, plaintiffs/appellees vs. Ford Motor Company et al., defendants/appellants.  
**Opinion by:** Ostrander, C.J. (court membership listed in secondary sources).

**Public access points (finding aids / digitized primary):**
- Full opinion available via Justia and Michigan case law aggregators.
- Michigan Supreme Court records and briefs (1850–2011) digitized by Wayne State University Arthur Neef Law Library (IMLS grant); full-text searchable on Google Books and LLMC Digital. Search by Michigan Reports citation, docket number, or party names.
- Google Books entry: *Dodge v. Ford Motor Co., 204 MICH 459 (1919): Transcript of Testimony taken before Samuel L. May, Circuit Court Commissioner* (Michigan Supreme Court Records and Briefs series; digitized from Arthur Neef Law Library). Contributors include counsel Elliott G. Stevenson, William L. Carpenter, Alfred Lucking, Alexis C. Angell, and Henry Ford.
- Chapman Law Review (Vol. 17, No. 2, 2014) compilation by Linda Kawaguchi reproduces/transcribes key historical documents and notes the Michigan Supreme Court Record exceeds 800 pages, containing trial transcripts (18 witnesses, May 21–June 7, 1917), pleadings, directors’ minutes, financial statements, contracts, letters, and photographs. Source of the Record: Thomas M. Cooley Law Library (Lansing) collection of Michigan Supreme Court records and briefs; also Wayne State holdings.
- Docket numbering note: Gap card lists “Docket No. 58”; published opinion headers and secondary citations use “No. 47”. Exact docket assignment requires inspection of the original record (not performed here).

**Corroborative value for executive dynamics:** The Record (as described in secondary scholarly finding aids) is the primary evidentiary source for contemporaneous witness testimony, board minutes, and financial data underlying the shareholder-vs-management conflict. No claim is made here as to specific box/folder contents beyond what public finding aids state.

## BFRC Accession 1 – Henry Ford Office Papers / Minority Context

**Clarification from public finding aids (Benson Ford Research Center / The Henry Ford):**
- Accession 1 is the Fair Lane Papers subgroup (Henry and Clara Ford personal and family papers, 1835–1950s bulk). Organized into Personal and Family papers series, Public Affairs materials series, and Business Affairs materials series. Finding aid PDF: http://www.dalnet.lib.mi.us/henryford/docs/FairLanePapersSubgroup_Accession1.pdf (linked from BFRC overviews).
- “Henry Ford Office Papers” are a separate multi-accession group (Highland Park Office records subgroup; Engineering Laboratory Office records subgroup; etc.). Not synonymous with Accession 1.
- No public finding aid located that designates Accession 1 (or any single accession) as “*Henry Ford Office Papers — Minority*” specifically for minority-shareholder litigation files.
- Closely related public collection: Accession 96 – John F. Dodge Estate Trust Lawsuit collection (1902–1928, bulk 1925–1928). Finding aid: http://www.dalnet.lib.mi.us/henryford/docs/JohnFDodgeEstateTrustLawsuitCollection_Accession96.pdf. Contains financial statements, stock-related materials, and documents assembled for post-1919 tax litigation stemming from the 1919 minority buy-out. Preferred citation form given in the aid.
- Highland Park Office / Records Stored in 1919 series (Accession 2) covers earlier office correspondence (to ~1919) but is not Accession 1.

**Corroborative value:** Public aids confirm BFRC holds extensive Ford Office and related personal papers that may contain contemporaneous material on the 1916–1919 shareholder dispute and buy-out. Exact location of any “minority” files requires on-site use of the published finding aids and box lists; none are asserted here.

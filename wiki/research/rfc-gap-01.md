### Repository confirmation [1]

The Henry Ford AskUs guide “Executives” (updated 17 Dec 2025, answered by Kathy M) lists Charles Sorensen key resources as:

- Acc. 38 Charles Sorensen Office Files Personal Records
- Acc. 65 Owen W. Bombard Interviews series * box 66-69 Sorensen
- *My Forty Years with Ford.* Charles E. Sorensen

That is the same accession/box range named on the GAP-01 card. Acc. 65 is the Owen W. Bombard interviews series, 1951–1961, held at the Benson Ford Research Center.

### What Acc. 65 is [2][3]

Owen W. Bombard (b. 1922) trained in Allan Nevins’s Oral History Research Office at Columbia, then was hired by Ford Motor Company Archives in 1950 to run an Oral History Section. Stated purpose (Bombard, 1952 AHA / *American Archivist* “A New Measure of Things Past”): increase quantity and quality of sources and reduce “blank spots.” Project wound down 1957.

Digitized Acc. 65 siblings (e.g. C. F. Holton, F. Bennett) carry the series boilerplate: interviewer’s questions omitted from the account; narrative is the donor’s language after the donor reviewed and signed the manuscript. INFERRED: CES boxes 66–69 follow the same editorial protocol. That already differs from a raw tape.

### Relationship to the 1956 book [4]

David L. Lewis, introduction to the 2006 Wayne State reprint of *My Forty Years with Ford*:

- Reminiscences were taped at the Ford Archives in the early 1950s by Owen Bombard.
- Sorensen praised Bombard’s “complete and accurate Ford chronology.”
- At the time of the tapings Sorensen planned an autobiography covering every aspect of his Ford career.
- Published New York: W. W. Norton, 1956, with Samuel T. Williamson. 345 pp.

VERIFIED: the book is a Williamson-shaped commercial narrative built on the Bombard tapings, not a print of boxes 66–69.

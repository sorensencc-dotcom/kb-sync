# GAP-28 Findings: Mead Bricker (Ford Executive, Willow Run, WWII production)

## Summary
Public secondary sources and institutional finding aids corroborate the core biographical claims for Mead L. Bricker as a long-term Ford production executive who managed key wartime manufacturing, including Pratt & Whitney engines, Sperry gun directors, and the Willow Run B-24 plant. No primary documents (interviews, corporate memos, or dockets) were accessed or read in full during this remote research; verdict remains PARTIAL.

## Corroborated Claims
- Career span and roles: Joined Ford 1904 as machinist (left, returned 1914); toolmaker/experimental; superintendent roles under Charles Sorensen (tractor plant 1917, Rouge production superintendent 1920+, Highland Park); 1940– directed Aircraft Engine Plant at Rouge for Pratt & Whitney engines; supervised Sperry anti-aircraft gun director production at Highland Park; Manager/General Manager of Willow Run bomber plant from ~1942 through WWII; appointed Vice President in charge of manufacturing 1945; retired 1950 (remained director/consultant). (The Henry Ford AskUs / Benson Ford Research Center personality summary; NYT 30 Jan 1964 obituary).
- Willow Run management: By autumn 1943, top leadership at Willow Run passed from Charles E. Sorensen to Mead L. Bricker. Plant achieved peak rates of one B-24 per hour (or every 63 minutes) in 1944. (Wikipedia "Willow Run"; multiple secondary accounts including Assembly Magazine, Warfare History Network).
- Death and life dates: Born Youngstown, Ohio; died 29 Jan 1964 (Royal Oak, Mich.) at age 78 after 46-year career; retired 1950. (NYT obituary). Gap card's "1914–1950" aligns with primary Ford employment window, not lifespan (actual ~1885/86–1964).

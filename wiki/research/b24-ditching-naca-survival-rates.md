### NACA Wartime Reports — Ditching Tests

#### L-648 (MR L4K14) — Full-Scale B-24D James River Test
- Margaret F. Steiner, Langley, Nov 1944.  
- Instrumented ditching: max longitudinal deceleration **2.7 g**, normal **1.7 g**, transverse **0.5 g**.  
- Bottom pressures frequently exceeded recorder limits (**>30–50 lb/in²**).  
- Contact speed ~114 ft/s, flaps down, high attitude; brief skip then plow-through.  
- NTRS 19930092642; UNT Digital Library.

#### L-620 (MR L5D07) — 1/16-Scale Dynamic Model Tests
- Lloyd J. Fisher & Margaret F. Steiner, Apr 1945.  
- Tests in Langley Tank No. 2 (calm) and outdoor catapult (calm + rough water).  
- **Preferred technique**: lowest practical vertical and horizontal velocity; medium 4–5° attitude, flaps down; parallel to waves when possible.  
- Expected decelerations **~5 g**.  
- Hydroflap (narrow trapezoidal) most effective against diving.  
- Simulated bottom damage (nose window, nose-wheel doors, bomb-bay doors) strongly increased nosing-in / diving.  
- Model settled to wing level rapidly; damaged B-24 judged unsafe.  
- NTRS 19930093659; DTIC ADB815061.

#### L-617 (MR L6A03) — Model / Full-Scale Correlation
- Attitudes and displacements agreed reasonably; full-scale peak longitudinal deceleration ~50 % higher than corresponding model run.

### B-24 Ditching Survival Statistics (European Theater)
From 8th Air Force Statistical Summary (1942–May 1945) / RAF Air-Sea Rescue data (ditch + bailout over water):

| Type | Crew involved | Rescued | Rate |
|------|---------------|---------|------|
| B-24 | 1,025 | 272 | **26.5 %** |
| B-17 | 3,336 | 1,266 | **37.9 %** |
| All 8th AF | 4,588 | 1,633 | ~35.6 % |

- Strong seasonal effect (summer rescues ~60 %, winter ~15 %; cold-water exposure).  
- Structural contributors: high-mounted Davis wing concentrates load on fuselage; frequent break-up at bomb bay or #6 bulkhead; bomb-bay doors collapse; top turret drops onto flight deck; rapid sinking (average float time cited ~8 min).  
- One sample of 41 B-24 ditchings: ~35 % of crew killed on impact or failed to egress.  
- Post-D-Day comparison: ~62 % of B-24s broke into two or more sections vs ~22 % of B-17s; drowning rates ~24 % (B-24) vs ~6 % (B-17).  
- Late-war mitigations (bomb-bay stiffening ribs, additional escape hatches, improved training) produced only marginal improvement.

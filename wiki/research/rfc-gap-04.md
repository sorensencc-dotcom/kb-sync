### Findings Summary
### Findings Summary

Kanzler’s 1943–44 role is real and documented at the level of office and kinship. His specific claims about Clara, Bennett, Sorensen, Navy release, and “we talked a lot” with HFII remain **single-source or secondary**. The 1926 Model T memo is the wrong year. There is no open BFRC fonds titled “Kanzler 1943–44 letters.” Test him against WPB files, Acc. 975, Campsall, and Acc. 38 — not against Sorensen’s book.

### What is verified

Employment window at FMC: 1916–1926. Brother-in-law of Edsel (married Josephine Hudson Clay, Eleanor Ford’s sister). Forced out after the 26 Jan 1926 eight-page memo to Henry Ford urging replacement of the Model T (BFRC Acc. 1025 / Fair Lane; object THF97068). [1][2]

1942–44 public office: chief of the automotive branch of the War Production Board, later director general. NYT obituary 12 Dec 1967. Contemporary press (e.g. 1942 appointments) matches. [3]

26 May 1943 Edsel dies. 1 Jun 1943 stockholders meeting with Henry, HFII, Benson present. Henry resumes presidency. HFII released from Navy and elected VP 15 Dec 1943 (standard chronology). [4]

THF official key resources for Kanzler stop at:

- Acc. 975 Nevins and Hill Interviews series, box 1, Ernest Kanzler
- Acc. 1025 1926 memo

No Acc. number for a 1943–44 Kanzler incoming/outgoing file. [1]

### Claims that are still single-source (the card’s target)

From later Kanzler talk and historians who used him:

1. He went to Navy Secretary Frank Knox and asked that HFII be released so the company would not be nationalized or parked under Studebaker. (American Heritage 2003; treat as Kanzler-derived until a Knox/Navy memo is found.) [5]
2. “We talked a lot” with HFII after the return; he was a principal adviser; he suggested Ernest Breech in 1946. (NYT obit quoting Kanzler.) [3]
3. After Edsel’s death Clara’s hostility to both Sorensen and Bennett became implacable. (American Heritage 1962, attributed to Kanzler.) [6]
4. Henry Ford feared HFII would fall under Kanzler’s influence and therefore wanted operations vested in others until the grandsons were older. (I. A. Capizzi recollection, same 1962 piece — a second witness, still decades later.) [6]

Sorensen 1956 is an adverse witness on Kanzler (1920 Sunday tirade; “coming between” Edsel and the parents) and cannot corroborate Kanzler’s 1943–44 self-account. [7]

### What not to do

Do not treat Acc. 1025 (1926) as evidence for 1943. Do not treat NYT 1967 or American Heritage 1962/2003 as primary. Do not use Sorensen ch. 3/20 as a referee on Kanzler’s character for this gap.

**Web additions:**

- Willow Run was an Albert Kahn-designed WWII bomber plant near Ypsilanti, Michigan, built by Ford in 1941 for mass production of the B-24 Liberator; at its peak in 1944 it produced a B-24 every hour, and Ford produced half of the 18,000 total B-24s at Willow Run. Source: Detroit Historical Society, "Willow Run" — https://www.detroithistorical.org/learn/online-research/encyclopedia-of-detroit/willow-run
- Engineer Charlie Sorensen implemented his pioneering model for just-in-time manufacturing to hasten production at Willow Run; the plant employed 40,000 workers and rolled a B-24 off the line per hour. Source: YpsiReal, "Fun Facts About The Willow Run Bomber Plant" — https://www.ypsireal.com/blog/post/fun-facts-about-the-willow-run-bomber-plant/
- Ford's own wartime film "The Story of Willow Run" states a new B-24 rolled off the line every 55 minutes, and that by 1944 Ford was rolling a Liberator off the line every 63 minutes, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Source: Internet Archive, "Building the B-24 Bomber During WWII — Story of Willow Run" — https://archive.org/details/74182StoryOfWillowRun
- **Open research point — production rate:** sources variously cite one B-24 every 55 minutes (Ford film), every 63 minutes, and one per hour at peak. Flagged for the Source Conflict Log; not resolved here.

**Web additions:**

- The post's table figures check out against the Ford WWII production database (sourced to Timothy J. O'Callaghan, "Ford in the Service of America," McFarland 2009): 282,354 Jeeps total including 277,896 GPW models with Willys engines; 17,008 aircraft drop fuel tanks; 5,475 aircraft rate-of-climb indicators; 1,894 B-24 knock-down kits. Source: U.S. Auto Industry in World War Two, "Ford Motor Company" — https://usautoindustryworldwartwo.com/ford.htm
- History.com: Willow Run produced 8,685 B-24s in total; Ford also made 57,851 aircraft engines, 277,900 Jeeps, 93,217 trucks, 2,718 tanks and 26,954 tank engines; Edsel Ford and Charlie Sorensen began mobilizing the Willow Run project in spring 1941. Source: HISTORY, "How Detroit Factories Retooled During WWII to Defeat Hitler" — https://www.history.com/articles/wwii-detroit-auto-factories-retooled-homefront
- The commonly cited split is 6,972 complete B-24s built at Willow Run plus 1,893 knock-down kits for assembly elsewhere, out of 18,482 B-24s total; peak month April 1944 produced 428 B-24s, and 100 completed bombers flew away between April 24–26, 1944. Source: World War 2 in Review, "Willow Run: Ford Motor Company Aircraft Plant" — http://worldwar2inreview.blogspot.com/2025/01/willow-run-ford-motor-company-aircraft.html
- **Open research points — B-24 totals:** the post's table says 8,685 B-24s, but sources split as 6,972 complete + 1,893 kits = 8,865, while another source says 6,790 complete, and another says 6,792 complete + 1,893 kits = 8,685. The complete-airframe figure (6,972 vs 6,792 vs 6,790) and the total (8,685 vs 8,865) both disagree across sources. Flagged for the Source Conflict Log; not resolved here.
- **Open research point — engine total:** the post's table reads 57,581 aircraft engines vs History.com's 57,851 — a possible digit transposition in one source, or an OCR misread of the table. Flagged as OPEN / UNVERIFIED; not resolved here.

**Web additions:**

- Alfred J. Lepine was Edsel Ford's secretary from 1918 until Edsel's death — confirmed in the Frick Collection archives directory description of the Edsel B. Ford office papers. Source: Frick Collection, "Archives Directory for the History of Collecting in America" (Edsel Ford) — https://research.frick.org/directory/viewItem/1307
- The Owen W. Source: The Henry Ford, "Finding Aid for the Nevins and Hill interviews series, 1952-1960" — https://findingaids.thehenryford.org/xtf/data/pdf/SE-003_NevinsAndHillInterviewsSeries.pdf
- The Bombard series covers Sorensen directly in other interviews — e.g. the Al Esper reminiscence (Oct 1951) contents list includes "Henry Ford, Col. Sorenson and Harry Bennett" — so the series is a live source for the Sorensen story, not just Edsel's. Source: The Henry Ford / CONTENTdm, "The Reminiscences of Mr. Al Esper" — http://cdm15889.contentdm.oclc.org/utils/getdownloaditem/collection/p15889coll2/id/5185/filename/5063.pdfpage/mapsto/pdf/type/compoundobject/cpdtype/document-pdf/show/5062

**Web additions:**

- *My Forty Years with Ford* was first published in 1956 (W. W. Norton, 345 pp.) and reissued in paperback by Wayne State University Press in 2006; described as "an unflinching eyewitness account of the Ford story," covering the Model T, the Dodge brothers conflicts, the five-dollar day, and Sorensen's development of the moving assembly line. Source: Google Books, "My Forty Years with Ford" — https://books.google.com/books?id=fv9WPvAXpGMC&printsec=frontcover
- The original 1956 edition was reviewed in Business History Review (Cambridge): "My Forty Years with Ford. By Charles E. Sorensen, with Samuel T. Williamson. New York, W. W. Norton and Co., 1956. Pp. 345. $5.95." Source: Cambridge Core, Business History Review — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-history-review/article/abs/my-forty-years-with-ford-by-charles-e-sorensen-with-samuel-t-williamson-new-york-w-w-norton-and-co-1956-pp-345-595/C3C29CF194ADAF56B55885A4BBC087FC
- Sorensen's memoir is also the source for the detail that his lifelong passion for the water stemmed from his Buffalo childhood rafting on the Niagara River, and that he was commodore of the Detroit Yacht Club. Source: Nailhed, "Broken Toys of the Motor City's Nobility" — http://www.nailhed.com/2015/01/broken-toys-of-motor-citys-nobility.html
- **Open research point — Sorensen's US arrival year:** the Google Books author bio says Sorensen was born in Copenhagen and "arrived in the United States in 1883," while the documentary's working Act 1 frame says he arrived in 1898. The two dates disagree by 15 years. Flagged for the Source Conflict Log; needs a primary check (immigration/census records) before the film states a year.

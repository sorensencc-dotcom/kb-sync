### Editorial stack (why “transcript vs book” is the wrong binary) [2]

Bombard, “A New Measure of Things Past,” *American Archivist*:

1. Tape
2. Transcript (narrative, questions omitted)
3. Interviewer marginalia (correct / clarify / elaborate)
4. Donor correction and editing
5. Topical or chronological rearrange, proof, permanent paper, index, bound signed copy

Digitized Acc. 65 siblings (Holton, Bennett, Orberg) print that boilerplate. INFERRED: CES folders follow it.

Then a sixth generation: Samuel T. Williamson shapes the signed reminiscence into Norton 1956, 345 pages (20 chapters). David L. Lewis (2006 intro): book is “based largely on his oral reminiscences” taped by Bombard; CES intended an autobiography covering every aspect of the Ford career. [3]

So the comparison set is at least:

tape? → transcript → draft folders (66–69) → final folders (66–69) → bound signed copy (if one exists outside these boxes) → Williamson 1956.

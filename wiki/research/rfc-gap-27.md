### Findings Summary
# GAP-27 Findings: Frank "Mike" Bennett (Ford, Russia 1930)

## Summary
Secondary historical sources consistently identify Frank Bennett (also spelled Bennet in some archival citations) as a Ford Motor Company engineer/technician/production manager dispatched to the USSR in late 1929 / early 1930 under the May 31, 1929 technical assistance agreement. He advised on startup of the first Soviet Ford assembly operations.

## Key Corroborating Facts (from public sources)
- Sent by Ford in December 1929. Over the course of 1930 he helped put assembly shops into operation in Moscow and Nizhnii Novgorod (Nizhny Novgorod / Gorki area) that assembled knocked-down Model A cars and AA trucks from Ford kits.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="18" />
- On February 1, 1930, at the startup of Assembly Plant No. 1 in Nizhni Novgorod, Bennett commented: “This was the beginning of a new era for Russia.” A cable was sent to Henry Ford. He acted as advisor to the Russian superintendent; Russians reportedly lifted him in celebration. He was the primary (and for an extended period the only senior) Ford specialist on site for the early assembly phase.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="10" />
- His role is described as production supervisor/manager/advisor for the practice assembly lines while the main GAZ “Auto Giant” plant was under construction. He observed Soviet workers learning tool handling and mixed car/truck assembly.<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="25" />
- Primary cited source for his statements is Frank Bennett, *Reminiscences*, November 1954, p. 130 (and nearby pages), collections of Henry Ford Museum & Greenfield Village / Benson Ford Research Center, Dearborn, MI, Oral History Section (accession 65 referenced in secondary literature).<grok type="render_inline_citation" citation_id="10" />

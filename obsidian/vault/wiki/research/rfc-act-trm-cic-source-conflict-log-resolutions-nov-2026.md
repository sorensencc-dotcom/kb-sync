## 2. Evidence Grounding & Key Resolutions

### Entry 1 — Exit Date: March 2 vs. March 4, 1944
* **Verdict:** **RESOLVED**
* **Primary Evidence:** Full-text retrieval of Allan Nevins, *"Power Is The Prize"* (p. 18):
  > "...on **March 2, 1944**, he got his answer. It came through an intermediary: Henry Ford wished him to resign because he had been ambitious for the company presidency! Sorensen, doubtless furious, of course instantly offered his resignation. **Its announcement on March 4** was a shock to the whole industry, and to war-production heads in the government."
* **Resolution:** March 2, 1944 is the internal date Henry Ford's demand reached Sorensen in Florida via intermediary. March 4, 1944 is the public announcement released from Miami Beach.

### Entry 2 & 3 — "Cast-Iron Charlie" Nickname: Origin & Valence
* **Verdict:** **RESOLVED**
* **Primary Evidence:**
  1. *Foundry Origin:* Henry Ford bestowed the name during Ford's first decade for Sorensen's metal/cast-iron pattern innovations and core-registration techniques that allowed molds to withstand mass production.
  2. *Floor Valence:* Allan Nevins (*"Power Is The Prize"*, p. 3):
     > "As bluntly direct as a piledriver, he was an aggressive executive, whose **tyrannies in handling workmen won him general dislike as 'Cast-Iron Charlie.'**"
* **Resolution:** Both valences are historically authentic: technical pride in the foundry, executive harshness/tyrannies generating shop-floor resentment.

### Entry 6 — Bennett–Smith Altercation (One Blow vs. Two)
* **Verdict:** **LIKELY RESOLVED**
* **Evidence:** Roscoe Smith's oral history explicitly states: *"That was the only blow that was struck."* A full-text audit of Nevins' *"Power Is The Prize"* confirms that the altercation does not appear in the magazine condensation, tracing the competing "two blows" rumor to unnamed "others" in Nevins & Hill's unabridged volume 3 (*Ford: Decline and Rebirth, 1933–1962*). Smith's direct eyewitness testimony stands.

### Entry 7 & 17 — Willow Run Conception & San Diego Visit (Jan 1940 vs. Jan 1941)
* **Verdict:** **RESOLVED**
* **Primary Evidence:** Charles E. Sorensen, *My Forty Years with Ford* (1956), pp. 282–283, 314:
  > "Early in January, 1941, I met Edsel and Young Henry and Benson in San Diego, where we inspected the Consolidated bomber plant... and that night I sketched a plant layout capable of turning out by mass production methods one B-24 an hour."
  Corroborated by the U.S. government's initial approach to Ford in December 1940 and Nevins (p. 13), locking the Coronado Hotel floorplan sketch to **January 9, 1941**. The January 1940 date is definitively eliminated.

### Entry 10, 11, 13 & 16 — Willow Run & War Production Metrics
* **Willow Run B-24 Airframes (Entry 10):** **8,685 Total** = 6,792 completed aircraft + 1,893 knock-down kits shipped to Fort Worth and Tulsa. The 6,972 / 8,865 numbers reflect middle-digit transpositions (79 ↔ 97).
* **Aircraft Engine Total (Entry 11):** **57,851 aircraft engines** (Pratt & Whitney R-2800s built at the Rouge). The 57,581 figure is an OCR digit misread (8 read as 5).
* **Peak Production Rate (Entry 13):** **One finished B-24 every 63 minutes** (April 1944: 453 airplanes produced in 468 operating hours), colloquially rounded to "about one an hour."
* **1942 Baseline vs. Frost Reel (Entry 16):** Total 1942 Willow Run output was **56 complete aircraft**. The Frost reel's claim that 56 aircraft were ruined by hard steel die damage is debunked as uncorroborated folklore.

### Entry 12 — Sorensen U.S. Arrival: 1883 vs. 1898 (Act 1 Rewrite)
* **Verdict:** **RESOLVED (Correction to Act 1 Framing)**
* **Evidence:** Sorensen was born September 1881 in Copenhagen. His father Soren emigrated in 1883; the family followed in 1884/1885 when Charles was 3–4 years old. Apprenticed at Jewett Stove Works in Buffalo. The 1898 date marks his move with his father to Milwaukee, and 1900 marks the move to Detroit. He did *not* arrive alone at age 17 in 1898.

### Entry 15 — "Jeep Day" July 18, 1945 Geography
* **Verdict:** **RESOLVED**
* **Evidence:** MotorTrend (*75 Years of Jeep*, Part 3) confirms a two-day event: July 17, 1945 assembly line tour for 700 guests in Toledo, OH, followed by July 18, 1945 field demonstration at Sorensen's **Cesor Farms in New Hudson, MI**.
